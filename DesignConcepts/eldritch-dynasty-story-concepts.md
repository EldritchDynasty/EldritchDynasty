# ELDRITCH DYNASTY — Four Story Concepts

**Status:** exploratory. Nothing here is canon. The concept brief (v0.2.1) contains one of these
already; the other three are alternatives to it, not additions to it.

Historical concept comparison, reviewed for provenance on 22 October 2026
against `main` at `098bfe02`. The v0.2.1 and millennium references below belong
to that comparison; the current brief is v0.2.3 and shipped campaigns are
300/500 years, with a separate 33-year engine demo. Proposed milestones here
do not describe additional implemented systems or current open work.

**What the question was.** The player accumulates five things — **reputation, bloodline, family
members, heirlooms, money**. Those are the nouns. A story concept's job is to turn each of them
from a number into a *reason*, and to lay milestones along the thousand years so the player always
knows what they are next trying to be.

---

## 0. The principle these four are testing

Three parts do three different jobs, and confusing them is why long games drift.

| Part | Question it answers | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| **The frame** | *Why do I care?* | Register, promise, dread |
| **The spine** | *What am I chasing?* | Milestones, the nine Ages, the escalation |
| **The conversion** | *Why is this number a story?* | Reputation, blood, family, heirlooms, money |

A resource that does not convert into the spine or the frame is a scoreboard. The test for any
concept below is the same: **name a resource, and the concept must say in one line what it buys in
the story.** If it cannot, the concept is not finished.

The brief's existing concept — the creditor at the table in 2042 — is strong on frame and thin on
conversion. It tells you brilliantly why you care and only vaguely what money is *for* beyond
buying books. Each concept below fixes that differently.

Register discipline throughout, per the eldritch-story skill: frame and interlude prose is
Dunsanian, sample passages included. The Tale layer stays Rothfuss in all four. The concept does
not change the voice of the run — only what the run is about.

## Concept 1 — THE NINE QUIET NAMES

> **This is the true story of how the house learned what to call the thing it owed — told in nine
> names, of which the last one is its own.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Told by** | The last of the line, alone, reading aloud |
| **Told to** | Nobody in the room. Something is listening anyway |
| **Told when** | Across one night in 2042, one name per interlude |
| **The chronicle is** | The name-book. The only place the eight recovered names are written down |
| **Register** | Dunsanian and incantatory. The frame is the only layer that speaks the names |

### The idea

The Church of the Nine Quiet Names holds that the naming *is* the ward — say them nightly, in
order, and the thing stays where it is. The Church has eight of them and has had eight for eleven
hundred years, and has never admitted the shortfall. Your family is the only house in the world
that can recover the rest, because a name is not learned from a book. It is heard, and only the
blood can hear it.

Every Age recovers one Name. A Name is not a word — it is a **true property of the thing**, and
each one contradicts the last, which is what makes eight of them an escalation rather than a
collection.

### The nine Ages

| Age | Stage | The Name recovered |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wonder | **The Guest.** It was invited. It kept its terms exactly |
| 2 | Mystery | **The Witness.** It did not write the contract. It signed as witness. Someone else drafted it |
| 3 | Wrongness | **The Debt.** In the contract's language, the word for the party and the word for the obligation are the same word |
| 4 | Wrongness | **The Door.** It did not come to Ardwen. Ardwen was built on where it already was, and the founder knew |
| 5 | Revelation | **The Tenant.** It predates the language the contract is written in, and the contract is in three hands, two of them dead scripts |
| 6 | Incomprehension | **The Ninth.** There are not nine names. There are eight, and a place kept for one — and the family has been trying to fill a slot the liturgy left empty on purpose |
| 7 | Incomprehension | The correction fails. The kept place was never for the counterparty |
| 8 | Cosmic implication | The same eight names, in the same order, in a Braccish liturgy six weeks away, warding something that is not this |
| 9 | Silence | No name. The place is still empty and the pen is on the table |

**The ring.** In the prologue the player names the house, not the founder. The ninth name is that
word. It has been on screen since minute four and the player will not see it coming.

### Milestones

Eight, and they are the cleanest ladder of any concept here, because each Name is a **permanent
patch on the world**, not a lore entry:

- **The Guest** — the family may refuse one Church summons per generation without penalty
- **The Witness** — a new event class opens: the search for the drafter
- **The Debt** — the ledger's arithmetic changes, publicly, and rival houses notice
- **The Door** — Terra-affine work at Ardwen becomes possible and expensive
- **The Tenant** — the family can date documents nobody else can, which is worth money at Sarrow
- **The Ninth** — invalidates two of the four earlier patches. This is the Incomprehension stage doing real mechanical damage
- **The seventh** — invalidates the correction
- **The eighth** — the Braccish liturgy becomes purchasable, and useless, and the player will buy it anyway

Recovered names slot straight into the existing Ledger-clause machinery in §18/§20 of the brief.
This concept is a re-skin of that system with a much better hook, not a replacement for it.

### What the five resources buy

| Resource | Conversion |
|---|---|
| **Bloodline** | A name is *heard*, not read. Only a woken man of the blood can take one first-hand; everything else in the house works from copies, and copies decay |
| **Family members** | Hearing a name uncontained costs the hearer. Every Name in the book has a person's name beside it, and the chronicle decides whether that is recorded |
| **Money** | Foreign liturgies, translations, and the Colleges at Corran. Two of the eight are only reachable by buying a dead church's paperwork |
| **Reputation** | Three names live inside the Church's own rites. Only an **Eminent** house is ever in the room when they are sung |
| **Heirlooms** | The Regalia are containers. The Nine-Fold Seal holds names, and a lost Regalia is a *lost name* — the single most punishing heirloom loss in any concept here |

### Beauty, so the dread lands

The night the family first sings all eight correctly, in the hall, with everyone home. It works.
Nothing comes. It is the most beautiful scene in the game and it is a false summit — the player
will spend four hundred more years learning what they actually did that night.

### The risk

A collectible cosmic tier explains the god. **Guard:** every Name must *eliminate readings, never
confirm one*. The player finishes the run holding eight true things about the counterparty and no
model of it whatsoever. Names 6 and 7 exist specifically to destroy the model the first five built.

### Frame sample

> He had eight of them by then, and the eighth had cost his aunt her sight and the west stair its
> bannister, and he had them in order, which the Church holds is the whole of the ward. There was
> a place at the end where the ninth should go. It had been kept empty for a thousand years by
> people who had assumed that emptiness was the same thing as waiting.

## Concept 2 — THE TITHE

> **This is the true story of what the house paid, one name a generation for a thousand years —
> told by the last one left, whose own name is not on the list.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Told by** | The last of the line, reading a roll aloud, because reading it aloud is the terms |
| **Told to** | The list itself |
| **Told when** | 2042, one page per interlude, forty pages |
| **The chronicle is** | The tithe roll. Every generation the player writes a name into it |
| **Register** | Dunsanian, liturgical, and very short. Interludes are a name and a sentence |

### The idea

The 1042 contract does not come due in 2042. It comes due **every generation**, and it always has.
One of the blood, once a generation, and the house prospers. The family has been paying since the
founder and has told itself, in the chronicle, a thousand years of stories about drownings and
fevers and sons who went to sea.

The player chooses. Every generation, from the tree, by name.

### The nine Ages

| Age | Stage | What the Age establishes about the tithe |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wonder | The terms are gentle and the house prospers, visibly, immediately |
| 2 | Mystery | The chronicle says the first tithe was a servant. The parish roll disagrees |
| 3 | Wrongness | A tithed person's belongings stay. Their **name** does not — nobody can hold it in their mouth, and the chronicle entry is legible to strangers only |
| 4 | Wrongness | A tithe refused is not punished. The next generation simply produces one fewer child, and no rite or physic has ever changed this |
| 5 | Revelation | An older roll exists, in a hand nobody recognises, running back past 1042 |
| 6 | Incomprehension | It was never a payment. It was a **sample** |
| 7 | Incomprehension | It was not sampling the family |
| 8 | Cosmic implication | A schedule of other rolls, other houses, the same hand |
| 9 | Silence | The fortieth line is ruled and empty |

### Milestones

The ascension ladder becomes, exactly, **the project of producing a person the tithe cannot take**,
and each rung changes the terms rather than granting a bonus:

| Rung | What it buys against the tithe |
|---|---|
| **Touched** | The taking is slower. There is time to say goodbye, and the family starts doing so |
| **Adept** | It must be **named aloud** by the Head, in the hall. The house stops pretending |
| **Hierophant** | The family may **choose** rather than receive. This is the worst gift in the game |
| **Vessel** | Substitution becomes legal under the terms. Bought orphans, wards, hostages — and every one is a Discrepancy the Church will eventually prove |
| **Demigod** | It takes a **cadet branch** instead of a person, all at once, and the branch is chosen by something that is not the player |
| **God** | Nothing is taken. Age 9 is silent about what replaced it |

### What the five resources buy

| Resource | Conversion |
|---|---|
| **Family members** | Literal currency. The strongest resource-to-story conversion available in this design — a headcount that the player spends in people |
| **Bloodline** | Deep blood counts double. One of the hot line settles what two thin ones cannot. The optimisation is obvious and monstrous |
| **Money** | Substitutes, and the paperwork to make a substitute look like blood |
| **Reputation** | Cover. An Exalted house's dead are mourned, not investigated |
| **Heirlooms** | Burdens that hold a person in place for a year — a stay of execution with a price |

### Beauty, so the dread lands

**The tithe year is the only year the whole family comes home.** Cadets travel, daughters married
out return, the hall is full for the first time in twenty years, and there is music. That is the
signature scene, once a generation, and it is a party. The player will come to dread the music.

### The risk

Unrelieved grimness, and it competes with the Vessel rite in §22 of the brief — two rituals that
both consume a named relative is one too many. **Resolution:** the Vessel stops being an ascension
cost and becomes the *one time the family pays itself instead of paying out*, which is either
defiance or theft depending on which of the nine Ages you are in.

### Frame sample

> The list ran to thirty-nine names, and the fortieth line was ruled and empty, and the pen beside
> it had been set down by somebody with a very steady hand. Of the thirty-nine he knew perhaps
> nine, and of those nine he had liked two. It is a strange grief, to mourn in handwriting you
> cannot read.

## Concept 3 — THE TESTAMENT

> **This is the true story of how the house became a god that people are still arguing about —
> told by two of its priests, over the oldest copy of its book, in a language the family never
> spoke.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Told by** | Two scholars of a church founded on the family, deciding which passages are canonical |
| **Told to** | Each other, and a synod meeting in the morning |
| **Told when** | 2042, the night before the vote |
| **The chronicle is** | Scripture. The player is writing a bible and does not know it |
| **Register** | Dunsanian, exegetical, faintly bureaucratic. Two clever people arguing about a sentence |

### The idea

The Two Listeners in the brief's §16 already have the right agendas — one wants the record
accurate, one wants the family to survive the night. Make them **priests**, and the frame acquires
a function the creditor version does not have: the frame *interprets*. Every embellishment the
player made becomes a miracle somebody has to defend. Every omission becomes a mystery of the
faith. Every truthfully recorded atrocity becomes a hard passage that four hundred years of
doctrine has been built around.

The player is not being read to. The player is being **canonised**, and can hear the argument.

### The nine Ages

| Age | Stage | What the Age establishes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wonder | The contract reads like a covenant. Its language is liturgical, and beautiful |
| 2 | Mystery | Its phrasing predates the Church's own liturgy. Or the liturgy is the copy |
| 3 | Wrongness | Three of the Nine Quiet Names are family names, and were before 1042 |
| 4 | Wrongness | The ward works. That is the problem |
| 5 | Revelation | The Church was founded to contain a previous instance of exactly what this family is doing |
| 6 | Incomprehension | The containment succeeded. What the Church holds is not a god — it is a debt |
| 7 | Incomprehension | Correction: the Church is not the jailer. It is the collection office, and has been since 604 |
| 8 | Cosmic implication | The Ambric empire fell for this reason, and the same office destroyed the records |
| 9 | Silence | The synod votes in the morning. There is a ninth chair at the table |

This is the concept that uses the world document hardest — the Church older than the kingdom, the
liturgy warding a thing doctrine denies, the empire that fell in 604, and no printing press, so
that **the number of copies of your book is a real integer** and burning one is a real act.

### Milestones

Doctrinal, and they run in parallel with the ascension ladder rather than replacing it:

| Milestone | Gate | The frame's reaction |
|---|---|---|
| **First attested miracle** | An Adept, publicly witnessed | The scholars locate the earliest entry and disagree about it |
| **First shrine** | Regarded, and coin | Someone outside the family writes something down about you, unprompted |
| **First martyr** | A death recorded truthfully at Respect cost | The hardest passage in the book. Cannot be un-written |
| **First heresy** | A cadet branch with its own chronicle | Two scriptures. The synod holds both |
| **The canon closes** | Eight Named Arts | Nothing written after this date is admissible, including the truth |
| **Canonisation** | God | The frame stops arguing |

**Demigod Stagnation becomes schism** — aggrieved cadets with their own libraries and their own
chronicles is already in the brief, and here it is doctrinally load-bearing rather than a
discontent counter.

### What the five resources buy

| Resource | Conversion |
|---|---|
| **Reputation** | Congregation. Exalted is not prestige here, it is the point at which the rites become *public* |
| **Money** | Copies. Half a year of a scribe's life each, and every copy is a hostage to fortune and a defence against a fire |
| **Heirlooms** | Relics. A relic whose two histories disagree (§15 of the brief) is a doctrinal crisis with a date |
| **Family members** | Saints, martyrs, heretics. What a person *becomes* in the book outlives what they were |
| **Bloodline** | The god the book is about |

### Beauty, so the dread lands

The first time a stranger on the road — a Wayfolk singer, a hedge-priest, a child — recites
something the family wrote, correctly, back to them. Warm, astonishing, and the beginning of
losing control of it forever.

### The risk

Apotheosis concepts make the counterparty optional, and this one nearly does. **Guard:** the ninth
chair at the synod, and Ages 6–8, which exist to establish that the Church the family became is
the instrument the counterparty has been using since before the family existed.

### Frame sample

> They had been arguing since the middle of the afternoon about a single sentence written in 1388,
> which one of them held to be a miracle and the other held to be a girl who had drowned. Both had
> read it a hundred times, in the way that men read a thing they have already decided about.
> Neither had ever noticed that the ink of that entry was younger than the ink around it.

## Concept 4 — THE PROVING

> **This is the true story of the house, read back to the last of the line by the court that came
> to disprove it.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Told by** | A Church inquest, from the family's own chronicle, aloud, as evidence |
| **Told to** | The last of the line, who is the only surviving witness |
| **Told when** | 2042, over one sitting |
| **The chronicle is** | The exhibit. Every entry is admissible, including the blank ones |
| **Register** | Dunsanian and cold. Procedure described with enormous care, which is its own kind of dread |

### The idea

The purest expression of the Record / Omit / Embellish system the brief already has. The player
spends a thousand years building a defence for a trial, and — unlike every other concept here —
**the game tells them the trial is coming in the first hour**. That is the motivation engine:
everything you do is discoverable, and you know the date.

The twist is that the court is correct about every allegation, and the family's survival depends
on one lie held for four hundred years, and the player chose which lie in about the year 1600.

### The nine Ages

| Age | Stage | What the Age establishes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wonder | The contract is lawful. Notarised. The notary's name is in the parish book |
| 2 | Mystery | The notary's name is in the parish book twice, sixty years apart |
| 3 | Wrongness | A clause names the court that hears disputes. No such court has ever existed |
| 4 | Wrongness | The court exists. It has met. There are minutes, and they are indexed |
| 5 | Revelation | The minutes are older than the Church, in the Church's own archive, catalogued in the Church's own hand |
| 6 | Incomprehension | The family is not the debtor. The family is the **security** |
| 7 | Incomprehension | Correction: the family is not the security. The family is the **document** |
| 8 | Cosmic implication | A schedule of other securities, most of them discharged |
| 9 | Silence | The court rises. The ninth chair has not been drawn out and nobody remarks on it |

Age 6 is the concept's whole justification: six Ages of the player assuming they can *pay*, then a
clause establishing that nothing they own was ever theirs to offer. That is a genuinely good wrong
answer being broken, which is the hardest thing the escalation asks for.

### Milestones

Respect stops being prestige and becomes **jurisdiction** — which court can touch you:

| Tier | Who may try you |
|---|---|
| **Unknown** | The manor court. Anyone with land can hang you |
| **Known** | The magistrate at the market town |
| **Regarded** | The Warden's assize, which keeps records that survive |
| **Eminent** | The Church court alone, and it must give notice |
| **Exalted** | The Assembly, which meets every third year, and the delay is the whole prize |

Five milestones the player feels in their body, because at each tier the *class of thing that can
end the run* changes. This is the tightest fit with the systems the brief already specifies.

### What the five resources buy

| Resource | Conversion |
|---|---|
| **Reputation** | Jurisdiction, above. Standing is not honour, it is the right to a slower court |
| **Money** | Notaries, clerks, sympathetic archivists, and the industry in forged maternal records the brief already describes |
| **Heirlooms** | Exhibits. An heirloom with a false biography is a perjury with a date on it |
| **Family members** | Witnesses, who can be compelled, and who can predecease the summons |
| **Bloodline** | The thing being alleged. Deep blood is the charge sheet |

### Beauty, so the dread lands

The house's own archive: a cold room, a woman of the blood who has spent forty uncontested years
keeping it, and the specific pleasure of a family that is *good at this*. She is the best-drawn
character in the concept, she is the reason the run survives to 2042, and she has been dead for
sixty years by the time the court sits.

### The risk

Legalism reads dry, and it is the least beautiful of the four. It also risks demoting the
counterparty to a plot device. **Guard:** the ninth chair, unremarked, in every interlude, from the
first — and the clerk who set it there without ever deciding to.

### Frame sample

> The court had brought its own copy of the chronicle, which was a courtesy, and three others,
> which was not. Between the four books lay eleven hundred years of the family's carefulness, and
> it had taken one clerk a single morning to set them side by side. There was a ninth chair at the
> end of the table. Nobody had drawn it out and nobody remarked upon it, and the clerk who had
> placed it there could not afterwards recall having decided to.

## 5. Comparison

| | Nine Names | The Tithe | The Testament | The Proving |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Milestone clarity** | Best — eight patches | Good — six terms | Good — six doctrinal beats | Best — five jurisdictions |
| **Short-loop motivation** | Medium | **Highest** — every generation | Medium | High — everything is evidence |
| **Long-arc ambition** | High | Low — you are surviving | **Highest** — you are building | Medium |
| **Beauty** | High | Medium, and cruel | High | Lowest |
| **Fit with brief as written** | High — reskins the clauses | Medium — fights the Vessel | High — reuses the Two Listeners | **Highest** — it is the Record system |
| **Converts *money*** | Weakly | Well | **Best** — copies and shrines | Well |
| **Converts *family members*** | Well | **Best** — they are the currency | Well | Medium |
| **Converts *heirlooms*** | **Best** — a lost Regalia is a lost name | Medium | Well — relics | Well — exhibits |
| **Risk of explaining the god** | High | Medium | Medium | Medium |
| **Risk of tonal monotony** | Low | **High** | Low | High |

## 6. Recommendation

**Frame and spine are orthogonal, and the best run takes one of each.**

Take **The Testament as the frame** and **The Nine Quiet Names as the spine.**

The reasoning is the conversion table. The Testament is the only concept in which *all five*
resources convert cleanly — money buys copies and shrines, reputation buys congregation, heirlooms
become relics, family members become saints, blood becomes the god — and it is the only one that
answers *why accumulate* with something the player wants rather than something they fear. The Nine
Names is the best milestone ladder, and it drops into the existing nine-clause Ledger machinery
without changing a system.

They join at the seam, and the seam is the good part: **the eight names your family recovers are
the eight the Church already sings, and the family's project of completing the liturgy is
indistinguishable, from the outside, from the family's project of replacing the god the liturgy
was written for.** Ages 6 and 7 break both readings at once.

Fold the other two in as pressure rather than spine:

- **The Proving** supplies the last two Ages. The final century should be an inquest, and the
  brief already says the last Age draws from the harsh table. Respect-as-jurisdiction is worth
  stealing wholesale regardless of which concept wins — it is a better ladder than Respect-as-tier.
- **The Tithe** supplies the cost cadence. Not every generation — that is too grim to sustain for
  eight hundred years — but the *tithe year as the year the family gathers* is the single best
  scene in this document and it should exist in whatever concept ships.

### What to write next, in order

1. The prologue, and the house-naming, since the ring depends on it
2. The eight Names in full, and the two that invalidate the others
3. The first interlude and the eighth, which must be visibly different documents
4. The synod's two scholars, by name, with their disagreement written before either has a line
